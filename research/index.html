research

My research focuses on a central problem in autism and neurodevelopment: meaningful signals can vary across people, developmental stages, biological systems, measurement methods and datasets. I study which findings are robust enough to generalise, which differences are developmentally informative, and which can support better clinical interpretation and real-world pathways to assessment and support.

research agenda

Across behavioral data, clinical pathways, structural neuroimaging, transcriptomics and machine learning, I use the same underlying framework: characterise heterogeneity, test robustness, distinguish reproducible signal from context-specific effects, and evaluate whether the result has meaningful translational value.

This creates a research programme that links four levels of inquiry rather than treating them as separate topics: developmental and behavioral variation; molecular mechanisms and biomarkers; brain structure across development; and generalisation of computational models across heterogeneous datasets.

autism, development & clinical pathways

I study behavioral heterogeneity, early identification and the pathway from developmental concern to assessment and support. My work treats screening, diagnosis and service access as distinct stages and asks where delays, dropout and misinterpretation occur across the full pathway rather than attributing outcomes to a single screening instrument.

Current work combines behavioral secondary analysis with implementation-oriented research on early autism identification and family navigation.

molecular neurobiology, transcriptomics & biomarkers

I examine molecular heterogeneity in autism with particular attention to cortical transcriptomics, synaptic and immune-glial programmes, RNA regulation and dosage-sensitive neurodevelopmental mechanisms. A major concern is the gap between biologically interesting associations and evidence strong enough for clinical interpretation.

My biomarker work therefore emphasises replication, independent validation, clinically relevant comparator groups and whether proposed molecular signals add information beyond existing developmental assessment.

neuroimaging & developmental variation

Using multi-site structural MRI data, I study whether associations between autism-related phenotypes and brain structure change across development. The emphasis is on moderation, site heterogeneity, covariate control and sensitivity analysis rather than assuming a single age-invariant neuroanatomical phenotype.

The ABIDE I project applies this framework to subcortical volumes and autism symptom domains across a multi-site sample.

machine learning & cross-dataset generalisation

I use machine learning when it directly addresses a scientific question, particularly transfer learning, domain adaptation and evaluation across datasets with different feature spaces, distributions or populations. The main question is not whether a model performs well within one dataset, but whether it captures signal that transfers beyond the conditions in which it was trained.

This work prioritises external portability, transparent baselines and sensitivity to dataset shift over headline accuracy.

current research questions

  • How does autism-related behavioral, neuroanatomical and molecular heterogeneity vary across development?
  • Which transcriptomic and biomarker signals are reproducible enough to support meaningful clinical interpretation?
  • Which apparent findings remain stable across sites, cohorts, platforms and analytical choices?
  • How can machine-learning models be evaluated when datasets differ in features, distributions and populations?
  • Where do early autism identification pathways fail between first concern, screening, referral, assessment and access to support?

methods & data

Statistical and computational methods: statistical modelling, moderation analysis, machine learning, transfer learning, domain adaptation, dimensionality reduction, sensitivity analysis and reproducible analytical workflows.

Research approaches: secondary data analysis, evidence synthesis, multi-site analysis, mixed-methods implementation research and critical biomarker appraisal.

Data: behavioral and questionnaire data, structural MRI, transcriptomic and biomarker evidence, and clinical or observational datasets.

Tools: Python, R, SQL and reproducible research workflows.
