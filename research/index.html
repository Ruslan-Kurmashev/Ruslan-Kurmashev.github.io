research

My research examines autism and neurodevelopment across behavioral, clinical, neuroimaging and molecular levels. I am particularly interested in which findings remain robust across datasets, developmental stages and measurement systems, and which are sufficiently validated to support meaningful translation.

autism, development & clinical pathways

I study behavioral heterogeneity, early identification and the clinical pathway from developmental concern to assessment and support. A recurring principle is that screening, diagnosis and access to intervention are distinct stages, and that performance should be evaluated across the full pathway rather than through a single instrument.

molecular neurobiology, transcriptomics & biomarkers

I examine molecular heterogeneity in autism with particular attention to cortical transcriptomics, synaptic and immune-glial programmes, RNA regulation, dosage-sensitive neurodevelopmental mechanisms and the translational limits of proposed biomarkers. My work distinguishes mechanistic evidence from claims of clinical diagnostic utility.

neuroimaging & developmental variation

Using multi-site structural MRI data, I am interested in how age and developmental stage shape associations between autism-related phenotypes and brain structure. The emphasis is on moderation, site heterogeneity, transparent preprocessing and sensitivity analyses rather than treating autism as a single static neuroanatomical phenotype.

ABIDE I neuroanatomy project →

machine learning & cross-dataset generalisation

I use machine learning when it contributes directly to the scientific question, especially transfer learning, domain adaptation and evaluation across non-identical feature spaces or populations. A central concern is whether apparent predictive performance represents transferable signal or dataset-specific structure.

current questions

  • How does autism-related behavioral, neuroanatomical and molecular heterogeneity vary across development?
  • Which transcriptomic and biomarker signals are reproducible enough to support clinically meaningful interpretation?
  • How can machine-learning models be evaluated honestly when datasets differ in features, sites and populations?
  • Where do early autism identification pathways fail between first concern, screening, referral, assessment and support?

methods & data

Methods: statistical modelling, moderation analysis, machine learning, transfer learning, domain adaptation, dimensionality reduction, sensitivity analysis and evidence synthesis.

Data: behavioral and questionnaire data, structural MRI, transcriptomic and biomarker evidence, and clinical or observational datasets.

Tools: Python, R, SQL and reproducible data-analysis workflows.
