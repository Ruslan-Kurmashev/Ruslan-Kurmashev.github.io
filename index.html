Computational Biology · Autism Research

Ruslan Kurmashev, MD

Computational & translational researcher · Autism & neurodevelopment

I study heterogeneity in autism and neurodevelopment - how biological variation, developmental context and dataset differences affect what we can reliably measure, model and translate into practice.

My research connects three levels of the same problem: computational robustness across heterogeneous biomedical datasets, biological heterogeneity across molecular and neuroimaging signals, and clinical translation into earlier and more reliable identification and support pathways.

My background combines medicine, computational biology and applied biomedical data science, with additional work in scientific peer review, scholarly publishing and research-community development.

Research Programme

My work is organised around one central question: how can heterogeneous signals in autism and neurodevelopment be converted into findings that remain biologically interpretable, computationally robust and clinically useful?

Computational robustness

Transfer learning, domain shift, external generalisation and reproducible modelling across heterogeneous biomedical datasets.

Biological heterogeneity

Transcriptomics, neurogenetics and structural neuroimaging to examine convergent and developmentally variable mechanisms.

Clinical translation

Early identification, biomarkers and implementation pathways linking research signals to assessment, referral and support.
