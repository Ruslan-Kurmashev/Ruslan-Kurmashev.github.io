AutDB: Cross-Dataset Autism Movement Benchmark

A reproducible benchmark testing whether autism-related movement models retain useful signal when transferred between datasets with different feature spaces, task structures and distributions.

Zenodo preprintCodePresentations
Question

Does model performance survive transfer across genuinely non-identical autism-related movement datasets?

Design

Within-dataset baselines, leave-one-dataset-out transfer, domain-shift quantification and simple adaptation strategies.

Main contribution

Shows why internal accuracy and external portability are different problems in heterogeneous biomedical AI.

My role and contribution

My role spans study framing, cross-dataset evaluation, analysis interpretation, manuscript development and scientific presentation. I am first author on the preprint and presented the work orally at ISMB 2026. The project was completed with Adina Yessimova, Denis Traore and Olaitan I. Awe.

Research question

Strong performance within one dataset does not establish that a model will generalise to a different cohort, protocol or representation. This project asks how much performance survives when autism-related movement data are transferred across genuinely non-identical datasets, and which simple adaptation strategies improve or degrade portability.

Data and design

The benchmark uses two open, privacy-preserving datasets, MMASD and Engagnition. Their records were harmonised into a common metadata structure, including a proxy activity label derived from movement information represented through 2D skeleton dynamics and accelerometry. Evaluation separates within-dataset performance from cross-dataset leave-one-dataset-out transfer.

Methods

  • Logistic Regression and XGBoost as transparent tabular baselines.
  • Within-dataset IID evaluation and cross-dataset leave-one-dataset-out testing.
  • Wasserstein distances to quantify distribution shift in summary movement features.
  • UMAP to visualise joint feature-space structure.
  • CORAL covariance alignment and importance weighting as domain-adaptation baselines.
  • PCA-based representation to test whether a low-dimensional movement-intensity summary improves transfer.
  • TRIPOD-AI reporting elements and PROBAST-AI risk-of-bias framing to improve transparency.

Key finding

Within-dataset performance was not a reliable proxy for portability. Cross-dataset performance was substantially more fragile and was strongly affected by scaling, distribution shift and representation choice.

Basic covariance alignment partially recovered transfer performance for a low-level intensity proxy, while naive importance weighting was unstable and collapsing the movement descriptors to a single principal component degraded transfer performance.

Why it matters

The central contribution is methodological rather than diagnostic. Biomedical AI models are often judged primarily by internal performance, even when deployment requires transfer across sites, cohorts, devices or protocols. This project makes external transfer and domain shift explicit parts of model evaluation rather than treating them as secondary concerns.

Limitations and interpretation

The benchmark uses only two open datasets and a limited set of adaptation baselines. It does not establish a stand-alone autism diagnostic model. The results support cautious interpretation of model portability and motivate evaluation across genuinely heterogeneous datasets before claims of generalisability are made.
