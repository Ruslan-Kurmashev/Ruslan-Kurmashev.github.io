Structural MRI · ABIDE I

Developmental Variation in Autism Neuroanatomy

A multi-site dimensional analysis testing whether associations between autism symptom domains and subcortical brain volumes vary with age rather than remaining constant across development.

PublicationDOIPublisherABIDE I

Research question

Diagnosis-level neuroimaging comparisons can obscure variation within autistic populations. This study asks whether reciprocal-social, verbal-communication and restricted/repetitive symptom dimensions are associated with bilateral subcortical volumes, and whether those associations change with age.

Data

The final analytic sample includes 355 autistic participants aged 7–49 years from 16 ABIDE I sites. Phenotypic variables were drawn from the ABIDE I release, including three Autism Diagnostic Interview-Revised domains. Structural measures came from public precomputed FreeSurfer v6 derivatives.

Seven bilateral regions were defined a priori: amygdala, hippocampus, caudate, putamen, pallidum, thalamus and nucleus accumbens.

Methods

  • Cross-sectional secondary analysis restricted to autistic participants for dimensional symptom–morphometry testing.
  • ADI-R reciprocal-social, verbal-communication and restricted/repetitive scores standardised within the analytic sample.
  • Left and right FreeSurfer volumes summed to bilateral regional measures.
  • Natural-log transformation of regional volumes and total brain volume.
  • Adjustment for age, sex, total brain volume and site, with HC3 heteroskedasticity-consistent standard errors.
  • Separate Benjamini–Hochberg FDR control for 21 age-independent tests and 21 symptom-by-age interaction tests.
  • Leave-one-site-out analysis and multiple sensitivity analyses for robustness.

Main result

No age-independent symptom–subcortical association survived FDR correction. One age interaction did: the association between reciprocal-social interaction scores and bilateral caudate volume became more negative with increasing age (interaction coefficient −0.002933 per 1-SD symptom score per year; q = 0.044699).

The coefficient remained negative and nominally significant in all 16 leave-one-site-out analyses. However, the interaction was not supported in participants aged 18 years or younger and weakened when the upper age range was restricted.

Interpretation

The result is therefore treated as qualified evidence of cross-sectional age heterogeneity, not as a stable developmental trajectory or a general subcortical biomarker of autism. Site–age imbalance, the threshold-proximal corrected result, the pediatric null finding and the absence of independent replication all require cautious interpretation.

Status

The study was published on 30 December 2025 in Saudi Journal of Medical and Pharmaceutical Sciences, 11(12):1270–1281. DOI: 10.36348/sjmps.2025.v11i12.019. See the publication record or the publisher page.
