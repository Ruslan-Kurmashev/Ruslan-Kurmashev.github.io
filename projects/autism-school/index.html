Autism School

An early-support parent programme designed to provide structured, practical and evidence-informed guidance to families of young autistic children and families awaiting formal services.

MTU awardTITAN Health AwardsRelated evidence synthesis

Purpose

Autism School was developed around a practical service gap: families may have developmental concerns, a screening result or an autism diagnosis but still face a substantial wait before they receive formal support. The programme is intended to make that waiting period more useful by providing structured guidance that families can understand and apply.

Programme focus

The model centres on parent education, practical support and clearer navigation of early autism pathways. It is positioned as an early-support and educational programme rather than as a replacement for clinical diagnosis, multidisciplinary assessment or specialist intervention.

  • Structured, accessible guidance for parents and caregivers.
  • Evidence-informed information about autism and developmental support.
  • Practical strategies that can be used while families wait for formal services.
  • Clear distinction between screening, diagnosis, support and intervention.
  • Digital delivery designed to improve accessibility for families.

Research and evidence base

The programme is closely aligned with my broader work on early autism identification and service pathways. That research examines where families can be lost between developmental concern, screening, referral, diagnosis and access to support, and why screening alone does not guarantee timely clinical assessment or service access.

TITAN Health Awards 2026 — Silver Winner. The project received Silver recognition in Provider & Services — Digital Services for Children and Young People under the title Autism School: Digital Early Support for Families of Young Autistic Children in Ireland.

Position within my work

Autism School connects my clinical background, autism research and interest in implementation. It translates evidence about early-identification pathways into a practical support model while keeping clinical diagnosis and formal professional care clearly separate from educational guidance.
