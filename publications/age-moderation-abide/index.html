Age Moderation of Associations Between Autism Symptom Domains and Subcortical Volumes: A Multi-Site ABIDE I Study

Ruslan Kurmashev · Saudi Journal of Medical and Pharmaceutical Sciences

PDFDOIPublisherRelated project

Abstract

Subcortical morphometric findings in autism are heterogeneous, and diagnosis-level comparisons may obscure associations involving symptom dimensions and age. This cross-sectional secondary analysis examined whether Autism Diagnostic Interview-Revised reciprocal social interaction, verbal communication and restricted or repetitive behavior scores were associated with seven bilateral subcortical volumes, and whether age moderated those associations, using 355 autistic participants aged 7–49 years from 16 ABIDE I sites.

No age-independent association survived false-discovery-rate correction. One symptom-by-age interaction did: the association between reciprocal-social-interaction severity and bilateral caudate volume became more negative with increasing age (β = −0.002933, 95% CI [−0.004804, −0.001061], p = 0.0021285, q = 0.044699). The estimate remained negative across all 16 leave-one-site-out analyses, but the pediatric subgroup result was null and the association weakened when the upper age range was restricted. The finding is therefore interpreted as qualified evidence of cross-sectional age heterogeneity rather than a stable developmental trajectory or general subcortical biomarker.
