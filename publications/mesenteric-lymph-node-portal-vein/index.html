Mesenteric Lymph Node of the Small Intestine During Portal Vein Clamping (Лимфатический узел брыжейки тонкой кишки при пережатии воротной вены)

Ruslan Zh. Kurmashev, T. A. Amirkhan · VIII Russian Final Scientific-Practical Conference with International Participation of Students and Young Scientists, Avicenna-2017 · Novosibirsk · 13 April 2017 · pp. 500–501

Abstract

Structured summary from the published proceedings. The original conference paper does not provide a separately labelled abstract. This experimental morphology study examined changes in the mesenteric lymph node after temporary portal-vein clamping. The experiment included 133 outbred rats weighing 190–220 g. Portal stasis was induced for 6, 12 or 24 minutes, and animals were observed from 3 hours to 7 days. The study assessed how the duration of portal-vein occlusion affected edema, cellular composition, sinus structure and the transport function of the mesenteric lymphatic system.

Methods

Mesenteric lymph-node tissue was collected for histology after fixation in 10% neutral formalin, paraffin embedding and preparation of 5–7 μm sections stained with hematoxylin and eosin. The control group underwent laparotomy without portal-vein clamping.

Published findings

At day 7 after 6 minutes of portal-vein blockade, edema was reduced, primary and secondary lymphoid follicles were present and the lymph-node sinuses were widened, with reduced cellular density and hypertrophy of the reticular framework. After 12 minutes of clamping, capsular and parenchymal edema and diapedetic hemorrhage persisted, together with increased numbers of lymphoblasts and mature lymphocytes and fragmentation of the medullary cords. After 24 minutes, edema and cell density decreased, the sinuses remained markedly dilated and medullary-cord fragmentation persisted. The authors concluded that compensatory lymphatic function remained evident by day 7, despite structural changes caused by portal-vein occlusion.
