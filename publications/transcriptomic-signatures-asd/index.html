Transcriptomic Signatures in Autism Spectrum Disorder: Opportunities and Limitations across Brain, Blood and Saliva

Ruslan Kurmashev · American Journal of Pure and Applied Biosciences · 8(3):521–528

DOIPublisher

Abstract

This focused narrative review compares what transcriptomic studies can currently contribute to autism research across three specimen types: post-mortem brain, peripheral blood and saliva. Brain studies provide the strongest mechanistic evidence, with recurring synaptic, immune-glial, RNA-processing and mitochondrial themes. Peripheral and salivary studies offer greater clinical accessibility but remain constrained by small cohorts, pre-analytical variability and limited external validation. The review concludes that transcriptomics is currently most useful for mechanistic inference and candidate stratification, with potential future value as an adjunctive biomarker rather than a replacement for behavioural assessment.
