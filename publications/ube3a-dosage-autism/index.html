UBE3A Dosage Imbalance as a Molecular Framework Linking Angelman Syndrome and Dup15q-Associated Autism Phenotypes

Ruslan Kurmashev · American Journal of Medical Genetics Part B: Neuropsychiatric Genetics

DOIPubMed

Abstract

This narrative review uses UBE3A dosage imbalance as a molecular framework linking two neurodevelopmental phenotypes with opposite dosage directions. Loss of maternal neuronal UBE3A underlies Angelman syndrome, while maternally derived 15q11.2–q13 copy-number gains contribute to Dup15q-associated autism phenotypes. The review integrates genomic imprinting, neuronal ubiquitin biology, synaptic plasticity, developmental timing and therapeutic implications, emphasising that dosage, tissue context and intervention timing need to be considered together when translating UBE3A biology into clinical strategy.
